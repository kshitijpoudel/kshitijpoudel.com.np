Chapter 1

Overview & General Provisions

The Real Picture Assessment is how Global IME Bank determines the true financial situation of a micro or small business borrower — going beyond official documents to understand what's actually happening on the ground.

What is the purpose?

To collect and analyze information that determines two things:

Ability to Repay

Does the business generate enough profit to cover the loan installments?

Willingness to Repay

Does the client have a track record and character that suggests they will actually repay?

The 3 stages of credit analysis

1

Preparation — Client screening and loan application filling. Understand the basics before visiting.

2

Site Visit — Physical visit to business (and optionally household) to collect and verify information firsthand.

3

Analytical Processing — Build financial statements, calculate ratios, and cross-check the data for reliability.

The 4 credit analysis tools

#ToolEquivalent toPeriod coveredWho prepares it
1Assets & LiabilitiesBalance SheetAs of site visit dateLO calculates manually
2Income & ExpensesP&L / Income StatementLast 12 full months before visitLO calculates manually
3Financial RatiosRatio analysisBased on tools 1 & 2LO calculates manually
4Cross-checkingN/A (internal verification)Compared over timeLO calculates manually
⚠️

Critical rule: These tools are NOT the client's official financial statements. They are built by the LO from scratch using information observed and collected during the site visit. The client must never fill these in.

The Golden Rule: Conservative Approach

Applied throughout all analysis. When in doubt, be cautious.

⬇️ Minimize (Assets & Income)

  • Record assets at minimum fair value
  • If unsure of inventory value, take the lower estimate
  • Exclude receivables over 90 days old
  • Don't count cash the client refuses to show
  • If income is uncertain, take the lower figure

⬆️ Maximize (Liabilities & Expenses)

  • Include every liability, even informal ones
  • Use actual (not discounted) rent rates
  • Include all employees — formal and informal
  • Record expenses on accrual basis
  • If expense amount is uncertain, take the higher figure

Preparation for Credit Analysis

Before the site visit, the LO screens the client and collects the basic information needed to make the visit efficient and targeted.

Step 1: Client Screening (first meeting, max 15–20 minutes)

The LO has a structured conversation to assess if the client is even eligible before going further.

#TopicKey points to discuss
1Business activityIndustry type, location of business assets and sales points
2Ownership structureWho is the registered owner? Who are the actual decision-makers? How are responsibilities split?
3Business volumesApproximate total assets, liabilities, turnover, and net profit (official AND actual)
4Personal detailsAge, residence, household composition, previous banking history
5Loan requestPurpose, client's own contribution, suitable loan product and repayment schedule
6DocumentsPAN number, registration, licenses, identity documents, collateral documents
7Lending conditionsProduct terms, fees, sample payment schedule, expected process timeline
💡

The outcome of screening: both the LO (is the client eligible?) and the client (do they accept the conditions?) decide whether to proceed to a formal application.

Step 2: Filling the Loan Application

A deeper conversation happens while filling out the application form.

#TopicWhat to cover
1Business backgroundWhen did it start? What was the start-up capital? Key milestones and investments.
2Current detailsNumber of employees (permanent vs seasonal), seasonality by month, affiliated entities, contracts, lease agreements
3Managerial recordsWho keeps records? Paper notebooks, spreadsheets, software? Are records available?
4Future plansLong-term strategy, short-term goals, future borrowing needs
5Guarantor detailsRelationship to borrower, source of income, outstanding debts
6Cross-sellingIdentify other bank products the client may need

Document checklist (copies only of originals)

  • Citizenship certificate (original must be seen — no copy of a copy)
  • PAN registration certificate
  • Business registration certificate
  • Relevant licenses and permits
  • Property/collateral documents
  • Identity documents of guarantors
  • Previous loan agreements (if any)
  • IEE/EIA clearance if applicable
⚠️

Document rule: Always demand and verify original documents. Making copies from copies is prohibited for official documents (citizenship, PAN, license). For other documents (contracts), it's not recommended.

The full credit process (know this sequence)

1. Loan application submittedClient fills and signs the form with LO's help
2. CICL checkCredit bureau check for existing loans and payment history
3. Document collectionAll missing documents gathered before or during site visit
4. Site visit to businessPhysical inspection of all business locations
5. Site visit to household (optional)Mandatory for first-time micro borrowers
6. Collateral valuationIf collateral is required for the loan
7. Decision making (underwriting)Approval authority reviews and decides
8. Pre-disbursement documentsFinal paperwork required before releasing funds
9. Legal papers & disbursementLoan agreement signed, funds released
10. Post-disbursement monitoringFollow-up visits to verify loan use and repayment
⚠️

The LO can never promise or guarantee loan approval to a client. Always say conditions are "tentative" and refer to "Head Office committee" — never name specific individuals as the approval authority.

Chapter 3

Site Visit

The site visit is the core of real picture assessment. The LO physically goes to the business and collects all financial information directly — not through the client's self-reported forms.

Who must be present during the visit?

  • At least one person with full knowledge of the business state (owner/key shareholder/director)
  • Chief accountant or person who manages the financial records
  • Activity managers if the client runs multiple businesses or related entities

Primary requirements for the site visit

  • Visit must have clear goals and be completed efficiently — no unnecessary delays
  • Prepare a list of questions in advance based on the application information
  • All documents listed during the first meeting must be ready for the LO to review
  • If multiple business locations exist, ALL must be visited — unvisited locations cannot be included in analysis
  • Take photos of all business premises and key assets
  • For repeat clients: photograph proof of how the previous loan was used and how profit was reinvested

Objectives of the site visit

✅ Verify ongoing business activities visually

✅ Double-check earlier information provided by the client

✅ Collect commercial, financial, and managerial data as of visit date

✅ Evaluate how well the business is managed

✅ Build a socio-psychological portrait of the client

What to check and verify at the business

Conversation techniques during the visit

💡

Repeat questions in different words at different times during the visit. Consistent answers mean reliable information. Inconsistencies need follow-up.

If a client is reluctant to share real figures: explain that information is strictly confidential and used only for internal bank risk assessment.

Client behaviorWhat it may meanLO action
Exaggerates figuresMay be trying to deceive — inflate their caseVerify everything; apply conservative approach
Understates figuresMay fear disclosure to tax authoritiesExplain confidentiality; reassure and probe further
Refuses to show cashCould hide assetsDo not include that cash in the analysis

Site visit to household

When it is mandatory: First-time micro-borrowers who are the sole owner of their business.

What to collect:

  • Regular family/household expenses (food, utilities, rent, schooling, etc.)
  • Additional income sources beyond the business
  • Significant one-time spending — house construction, weddings, medical emergencies

Socio-economic analysis

Financial ability alone is not enough. The LO must also judge the client's character and willingness to repay.

Reputation check

Ask trustworthy third parties — neighbors, business partners, suppliers — about the client's reputation. A poor reputation in the community is a risk signal.

Payment discipline

Review past loan history (CICL report). Check for any defaults, delays, or informal borrowings that weren't declared.

Personal factors to observe and note

  • Age and health condition of the client
  • Education level
  • Attitude toward alcohol and gambling
  • Living conditions and stability of residence
  • Marital status; number and age of dependents
  • Severe health issues in the family
  • Business experience and background before this venture
  • Does the client reinvest profit in the business, or spend it personally?

Chapter 4 · Part 1

Assets & Liabilities

The Assets & Liabilities table is a snapshot of the business's financial position on the day of the site visit. Think of it as a business balance sheet, built by the LO from what they observed.

Core equation (always true) Assets = Liabilities + Equity
Equity (Net Worth) = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Current Assets — Section I

Assets that can be converted to cash within 1 year.

How transactions affect the table

TransactionWhat changesEquity?
Receive new loan (NPR 1,000,000)Cash ↑ 1M | Long-term liabilities ↑ 1MNo change
Buy goods with cash (NPR 1,000,000)Cash ↓ 1M | Inventory ↑ 1MNo change

💡

Key insight: Only selling goods at a markup increases equity. All other transactions are just shifting money between asset and liability categories — the owner's net worth stays the same.

COGS when markup is 20%: 360,000 ÷ 1.20 = 300,000. Profit (gross) = 360,000 − 300,000 = 60,000.

Chapter 4 · Part 2

Income & Expenses

This statement covers the last 12 full calendar months before the site visit. It shows whether the business makes enough profit to repay the requested loan.

⚠️

This is not the fiscal year (Baishakh to Chaitra). It is the 12 full months immediately before the site visit date — rolling 12 months.

Accrual vs Cash basis (critical concept)

Accrual basis (what we use)

Record income when goods are shipped/delivered, not when payment is received.

Record expenses when they are incurred, not when cash is paid.

Example

Goods shipped in March, payment received in April → Revenue counts in March.

Oil change every 9 months costs NPR 12,000 → Monthly accrual = NPR 1,333.

Revenue

Income from selling goods, products, or services through normal business operations.

Sources of revenue information:

  • Conversation with the client and key staff (sales managers, accountant)
  • Primary documents: bills, invoices, receipts, cash books, VAT filings
  • Managerial records: notebooks, Excel files
  • Cross-checking calculations (see Chapter 6)
💡

If the client has multiple business lines (e.g. wholesale + retail, trade + manufacture), separate the revenue by each activity for more accurate profitability analysis.

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

The cost of goods that were actually sold in the period — not just purchased.

In trade (most common) COGS = Net purchase price of goods sold (including customs duties if imported)
In manufacturing COGS = Raw materials + Direct labour (piecework) + Utilities per unit — for goods sold in the period
Using markup to calculate COGS (the standard method for LOs) COGS = Revenue ÷ (1 + Markup % ÷ 100)
Worked example

Revenue = NPR 1,000,000 | Markup = 20%

Revenue1,000,000
COGS = 1,000,000 ÷ 1.20833,333
Gross Profit166,667

Understanding Markup

Markup formulas Markup % = (Gross Profit ÷ COGS) × 100
Selling Price = Cost × (1 + Markup %)

Example: Cost = 100, Markup = 20% → Selling Price = 120
⚠️

Markup vs Margin — don't confuse them!
Markup is calculated on COST. Margin is calculated on REVENUE.
Markup 20% on cost of 100 → price 120 → but the margin is 16.7% (20/120).

Weighted Average Markup (when multiple product types)

Chapters 4.3 & 4.4

Ratios & Cross-checking

Financial ratios translate raw numbers into creditworthiness signals. Cross-checking methods verify whether the client's numbers are internally consistent and reliable.

The 5 financial ratios (memorize these)

1. Debt-to-Equity Ratio (D/E)
D/E = (Short-term Liabilities + Long-term Liabilities) ÷ Equity
✓ Recommended: less than 4.0
Measures how much of the business is funded by debt vs. the owner's own funds. Lower = safer. Above 4.0 = heavily dependent on borrowed money.
2. Liquidity Ratio (Current Ratio)
LR = Short-term Assets ÷ Short-term Liabilities
✓ Recommended: 1.25 or more
Can the business pay off its short-term debts from current assets? Too high can also mean capital is sitting idle — an opportunity to grow with a loan.
3. Net Working Capital / Loan Amount
NWC/LA = (Short-term Assets − Short-term Liabilities) ÷ Requested Loan Amount
✓ Recommended: 1.33 or more
Only used for working capital loans. Is there sufficient working capital to justify the loan size requested?
4. Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
DSCR = Net Profit ÷ Total Annual Loan Payments (all loans incl. requested)
✓ Recommended: more than 1.25
For term loans. Does the annual profit comfortably cover all loan installments? 1.25 means profit is 25% higher than total payments — adequate buffer.
5. Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR)
ICR = Net Profit ÷ Total Annual Interest (all loans incl. requested)
✓ Recommended: more than 1.5
For overdrafts and revolving credit lines. Can profits cover at least the interest charges with a 50% buffer?

Ratio calculation example — DSCR

Given: Net profit = NPR 500,000/year | Existing loan installment = NPR 200,000/year | New loan installment = NPR 160,000/year

Total payments = 200,000 + 160,000 = NPR 360,000

DSCR = 500,000 ÷ 360,000 = 1.39 Pass ✓ (above 1.25)

If DSCR were 1.10 → Fail ✗ (below 1.25)

Cross-checking Methods

Cross-checking verifies that the information collected is consistent and the numbers make sense together. The LO must identify any suspicious deviations and investigate.

Method 1: Equity cross-check (most important)

Tests whether the business's current net worth is logically consistent with what the owner started with and how much they've earned over time.

For new clients (first time being analyzed) Expected OE = Starting Capital (OE-0) + Accrued Profit + Investments made − Withdrawals taken

Accrued Profit = Average Annual Net Profit × Number of years in business
For repeat clients (analyzed before) Expected OE-2 = OE-1 (from last visit) + Accrued Profit (between visits) + Investments − Withdrawals

If period between visits is not 12 months:
Accrued Profit = Annual NP ÷ 12 × number of months
✅

Acceptable deviation: within ±20% between actual OE and expected OE. If the gap is larger, investigate further with the client.

Equity cross-check example

Started business 10 years ago with NPR 500,000. Annual net profit = NPR 200,000. No major withdrawals or investments.

Starting capital (OE-0)500,000
Accrued profit (200,000 × 10 years)2,000,000
Expected OE2,500,000
Actual OE (Assets − Liabilities)2,300,000
Deviation = (2,500,000 − 2,300,000) ÷ 2,500,0008% ✓

8% is within ±20% → acceptable. If actual OE were 1,500,000 → deviation = 40% → investigate.

What causes the gap? Common reasons

Actual OE < Expected

  • Hidden liabilities not identified
  • Undisclosed withdrawals (e.g. for house renovation, wedding)
  • Fixed assets over-valued in previous analysis
  • Profit was overstated (markup error, underestimated expenses)
  • Assets missing from current table

Actual OE > Expected

  • Undisclosed liabilities hidden to inflate equity (serious red flag)
  • Assets not counted in previous visit now counted
  • New investment by owner or new partner joined
  • Profit was understated (extra income not counted)
  • Fixed assets appreciated in value

Method 2: Cashbox–Sales check

Rough check that daily cash on hand is consistent with reported daily sales.

Cash at day-end + All cash expenses during that day = Annual revenue ÷ 365

Ask: "How much did you take in yesterday? The day before?" If the answer is very different from the declared annual revenue ÷ 365, investigate.

Method 3: Inventory flow check

Tests whether the inventory level today is logically consistent with purchases and sales since the last visit.

Inventory (today) = Inventory (last visit) + Purchases during period − COGS during period

(All values at purchase/cost price — NOT selling price)
💡

Today's inventory is higher than expected: Stock was overvalued today, OR client showed goods that don't belong to them, OR COGS was inflated (sales volumes actually lower than declared).

Today's inventory is lower than expected: Purchases were overstated, OR prior stock was over-reported, OR markup used in COGS calculation was wrong.

Method 4: Receivables check

Tests whether accounts receivable today are consistent with sales and collections during the period.

Receivables (today) = Receivables (last visit) + Sales during period − Cash actually received during period
Receivables check example

Receivables at last visit = NPR 160,000 | Sales this period = NPR 3,800,000 | Cash received = NPR 3,650,000

Receivables (last visit)160,000
+ Sales this period3,800,000
− Cash received(3,650,000)
Expected receivables (today)310,000

If actual receivables are within ±10% of NPR 310,000 → Reliable. Otherwise → investigate.

Tolerance thresholds — summary

Cross-check methodAcceptable deviation
Equity cross-checkWithin ±20%

⚠️

Any deviation beyond these thresholds means the LO must ask additional questions, dig deeper, and potentially revise the financial analysis. Large deviations that can't be explained = a serious credit risk signal.
